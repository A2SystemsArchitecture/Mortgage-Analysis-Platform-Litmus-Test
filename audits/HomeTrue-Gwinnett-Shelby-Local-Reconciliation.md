# Batch 1 local benchmarks: Gwinnett and Shelby

## Gwinnett County, Georgia

The [2026 Tax Commissioner schedule](https://www.gwinnetttaxcommissioner.com/pt/property-tax/billing-information/millage-rates) lists 34.86 mills for unincorporated Gwinnett. For a fictional $300,000 market-value home without exemptions, $120,000 assessed × 0.03486 = $4,183.20 annual ad valorem tax before fees or adjustments. The standalone scenario matched $4,183.20 using the published rate. This is an independent calculation, not a county-issued bill.

The main report's 0.95% planning input yields $2,850 annually before rounding, $1,333.20 below that benchmark. The main formula rounds the monthly amount to $238. The planning rate was not replaced with this single nonexempt comparison because eligibility, applicable levies, special charges and exemptions still need reconciliation. The county [homestead page](https://www.gwinnetttaxcommissioner.com/property-tax/available-exemptions) confirms the three S1R assessed-value deductions and states that applications are accepted year-round, with the assessment notice appeal deadline for current-year relief. The county note was updated accordingly.

## Shelby County, Alabama

The county's [historical Montevallo education example](https://shelbyal.com/DocumentCenter/View/200/How-to-calculate-property-tax---Shelby-County?bidId=) uses October 2012 rates. At $100,000 appraised and $10,000 assessed, 6.5 state, 7.5 eligible county and 37 other mills yield $510. The $4,000 and $2,000 H-1 deductions reduce the state and county taxes by $26 and $15, respectively, leaving $469. Both figures matched. This tests the calculation against published example figures without asserting that the rates are current.

Both tests passed alongside the existing cases. Current parcel and exemption reconciliation, main report totals and full market and state sign-off remain open. No deployment.
